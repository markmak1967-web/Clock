The need

The Clock is a family of driver instruments that read pre-2000 engine computers through their diagnostic link and present live data, warnings and maintenance guidance in the car. The first product replaces the MGF dash clock and reads the Rover MEMS 1.9 engine management system.

The data exists, but is locked away

Engine computers of the 1990s continuously measure coolant temperature, fuelling, idle, charging and faults. They share this only with a workshop scan tool plugged into the diagnostic socket.

The dash says very little

A temperature needle and a few warning lights are all the driver sees. Slow changes, such as a cooling system starting to struggle or an idle that creeps up, go unnoticed until they become a breakdown.

Standard tools don't reach these cars

Generic OBD-II scanners and apps can't read the MEMS 1.9. Owners of these cars have had no practical way to see their engine's data in real time.

The response: an instrument that fits where the dash clock was, reads the engine computer every drive, shows what matters, keeps a consistent record over time and turns it into plain-English advice for the owner and their mechanic.

What it does

  1. Replaces the original clockFits the clock opening from behind. Rest screen: a classic white-dial clock like the original, or a digital clock.
  2. Readouts of key engine dataCoolant, battery, intake air and throttle, with engine speed and manifold pressure rings.
  3. Indicators and warningsMaster caution on out-of-limit readings; fault codes in plain English; “clear and watch” to see whether a fault returns.
  4. Gear-change pointsCalculates the best change points for economy or for power, shown as a cue on the gauge.
  5. Trip computerDistance, engine running time and fuel used from the owner's fill-up entries.
  6. Motion sensing for wearRecords g-forces (acceleration, braking, cornering, bumps) as an indicator of suspension and component wear over time.
  7. Logs every driveKey readings and distances on every drive cycle. Location is never stored or sent.
  8. Analytics and adviceOpt-in car analytics page with trends and advice (“ask your mechanic to consider…”); a separate opt-in fleet page compares similar cars.
  9. OBD-II exportOver Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, so any OBD app can read the car.

Reads the Rover MEMS 1.9 over the K-line diagnostic link with a self-recovering connection. Read-only: never changes engine computer settings. Rover MEMS 2J is detected and reported; full support is in progress.

Your data

The rules the system is built to.

  • Vehicle location is never stored or transmitted. GPS is used for speed and distance only.
  • The car analytics page, the fleet analytics page and AI advice are each opt-in. With them switched off, no data leaves the car.
  • No driving scores. Advice concerns the condition of the car, not the driver.
  • The car analytics page is private to one car. Fleet outputs show totals and averages only, never another car's details.
  • Owners can delete their data at any time from their car analytics page.
